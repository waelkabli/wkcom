> *This post is a translation of the original Arabic article.*

Health transformation is changing the customer that [**digital health companies**](https://www.waelkabli.com/ar/blog/when-everyone-has-ai) sell to, and the outcome that customer expects.

Over the past several years, many companies built their offerings around the product itself: an app, a platform, a [**virtual consultation**](https://www.waelkabli.com/ar/blog/building-cura-lessons) service, a monitoring device, or a data dashboard. As [**health clusters**](https://www.health.sa/ar/clusters/list) mature, the buyer becomes responsible for the health of an entire regional population — for their access to care, the continuity of that care, and the efficiency of spending on them.

That buyer is purchasing an operational and health outcome.

The main question becomes: what outcome does this product improve within the patient journey?

The [**outcome**](https://www.health.sa/ar/moc-forum) might be reducing time to physician access, raising early detection of diabetes and hypertension, increasing referral completion rates, improving chronic disease control, or reducing avoidable emergency visits.

Technology delivers its value when it enters a clearly defined journey.

[**Virtual consultations**](https://www.waelkabli.com/ar/blog/building-cura-lessons) serve fast access and follow-up. [**Remote monitoring**](https://www.who.int/ar/news/item/20-03-1446-boosting-digital-health-can-help-prevent-millions-of-deaths-from-noncommunicable-diseases) serves chronic disease and home care. [**Artificial intelligence**](https://www.waelkabli.com/ar/blog/ai-in-digital-health) serves documentation, triage, and risk detection. Referral platforms serve transitions between primary and specialist care. [**Insurance and claims systems**](https://www.waelkabli.com/ar/blog/when-everyone-has-ai) connect service delivery to financing.

Every product needs to define which step it owns, who uses it, which decision it improves, and what outcome it measures.

Every company targeting health clusters needs clear answers to five questions:

1. Which patient population do we serve?
2. Which step in the care journey do we own?
3. Which health or operational outcome do we improve?
4. How do we measure the difference before and after deployment?
5. How does the solution integrate with existing systems, teams, and financing mechanisms?

Investors need the same questions in economic terms: who controls the budget? Who uses the product daily? What data proves the impact? What are the integration and operating costs? And what drives the customer to expand and renew?

Companies that can connect technology to the pathway, the financing, and the outcome will build deeper value. As health transformation matures, the market will shift toward buying [**operational capabilities that improve population health**](https://www.health.sa/ar/model-of-care).

The opportunity for founders is clear: build inside the care journey, and measure an impact that matters to the entity accountable for the population.
