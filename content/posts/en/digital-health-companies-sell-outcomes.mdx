> *This post is a translation of the original Arabic article.*

Health transformation changes the buyer for [**digital health companies**](https://www.waelkabli.com/en/blog/when-everyone-has-ai) and the result that buyer expects.

Many companies built their offers around the product itself: an app, platform, virtual consultation, monitoring device, or dashboard. As [**health clusters**](https://www.health.sa/en/clusters) develop, the buyer becomes responsible for the health of an entire regional population, access to care, continuity, and spending efficiency.

This buyer purchases a health and operating outcome.

The central question becomes: which result does the product improve inside the patient journey?

The result may be shorter time to a physician, higher early detection of diabetes and hypertension, more completed referrals, better chronic-disease control, or fewer avoidable emergency visits.

Technology creates value when it enters a defined care pathway.

[**Virtual consultation**](https://www.waelkabli.com/en/blog/building-cura-lessons) supports fast access and follow-up. [**Remote monitoring**](https://www.who.int/news/item/23-09-2024-boosting-digital-health-can-help-prevent-millions-of-deaths-from-noncommunicable-diseases) supports chronic and home care. [**Artificial intelligence**](https://www.waelkabli.com/en/blog/ai-in-digital-health) supports documentation, triage, and risk detection. Referral platforms support movement between primary and specialist care. [**Insurance and claims systems**](https://www.waelkabli.com/en/blog/when-everyone-has-ai) connect service delivery to funding.

Every product needs to define the step it owns, the user who operates it, the decision it improves, and the outcome it measures.

Every company selling to health clusters needs clear answers to five questions:

1. Which patient population do we serve?
2. Which step in the care journey do we own?
3. Which health or operating result do we improve?
4. How do we measure the change before and after implementation?
5. How does the solution integrate with existing systems, teams, and funding mechanisms?

Investors need the same answers in economic terms. Who owns the budget? Who uses the product every day? Which data proves impact? What is the cost of integration and operation? What drives expansion and renewal?

Companies that connect technology to pathway, funding, and outcome will build deeper value. As transformation matures, the market will purchase [**operating capabilities that improve population health**](https://www.health.sa/en/model-of-care).

The opportunity for founders is clear: build inside the care journey and measure an outcome that matters to the organization responsible for the population.
