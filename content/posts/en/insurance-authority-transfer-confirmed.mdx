At the end of the episode, I expected the transformation to redistribute mandates among existing institutions, including a transfer of health insurance roles from the Council of Health Insurance to the [**Insurance Authority**](https://www.ia.gov.sa/en/about-ia/about).

Effective [**4 March 2024**](https://www.ia.gov.sa/en/media-center/news/19), all health insurance mandates moved from the Council of Health Insurance to the Insurance Authority.

This development shows how large institutional transformations are built. The state defines the required functions and distributes them across regulation, funding, service delivery, and quality oversight.

The [**Insurance Authority regulates the insurance sector**](https://www.ia.gov.sa/en/about-ia/about). The [**National Health Insurance Center acts as the funding entity**](https://www.health.sa/en/model-of-care) and purchases health services for its beneficiaries. [**Health Holding and its clusters provide care**](https://www.health.sa/en/clusters). The [**Ministry of Health leads regulation and oversight of the health system**](https://www.health.sa/en/model-of-care).

Clear roles determine decision speed and [**execution quality**](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/quality-health-services). They also tell investors, providers, and technology companies which institution owns each decision.

Every company operating in health now needs precise answers:

1. Who regulates our activity?
2. Who purchases the service?
3. Who uses the solution?
4. Who measures quality?
5. Who owns the expansion decision?

[**Institutional transformation reshapes the market**](https://www.waelkabli.com/en/blog/founders-note-vision-2030) before its full effects reach citizens. Tracking the movement of mandates gives companies an early view of demand, contracts, and emerging opportunities.
