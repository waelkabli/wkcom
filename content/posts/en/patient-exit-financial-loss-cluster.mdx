When a patient leaves a health cluster, the exit sends a clear operating signal: the required service broke at the needed place or time. Under the [**target funding model**](https://www.who.int/activities/promoting-strategic-purchasing), that signal carries a financial cost.

When cluster funding becomes connected to the population it serves, the [**citizen experience**](https://www.health.sa/en/model-of-care) becomes part of operational and financial performance.

A cluster that provides the right appointment, completes the referral, and delivers the required specialty keeps the beneficiary journey within its network. When the service moves to another cluster or a private provider, the cost of care moves with it.

This creates a direct incentive to improve service.

[**Waiting time**](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/quality-health-services) becomes a financial indicator. Specialty availability becomes a competitive capability. Communication quality protects citizen trust. Pathway completion becomes a component of [**spending efficiency**](https://www.health.sa/en/model-of-care).

The purpose of this competition is stronger local service. Each cluster develops weak areas and allocates resources according to population needs.

[**Data**](https://www.waelkabli.com/en/blog/when-everyone-has-ai) becomes essential. The cluster needs to understand why patients leave, which services they seek elsewhere, where appointments face delay, which referrals stop, and which cases return to emergency care.

Each insight leads to an operating decision: expand a clinic, add a specialty, activate a [**virtual service**](https://www.waelkabli.com/en/blog/building-cura-lessons), contract with a private provider, or redesign an entire pathway.

[**Citizen satisfaction**](https://www.health.sa/en/model-of-care) becomes the product of the full system. Funding gives that result practical weight and turns beneficiary experience into a continuous leadership responsibility.
