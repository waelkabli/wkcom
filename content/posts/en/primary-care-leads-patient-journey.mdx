Health-system efficiency starts at the point that receives citizens first and directs their next step. This is the role of [**primary health care**](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/primary-health-care).

Primary care understands the beneficiary's health history, manages common conditions, identifies risk factors, directs cases that need specialist care, and follows them after treatment.

Strong primary care gives citizens timely access to the right service. Hospitals and specialist services can then focus their capacity on cases that need it most.

Primary care becomes the manager of the journey.

The primary care team needs a complete patient view, sufficient assessment time, referral authority, communication channels with specialists, and tools for continuous follow-up.

[**Digital health**](https://www.waelkabli.com/en/blog/ai-in-digital-health) expands this capacity. [**Virtual consultations**](https://www.waelkabli.com/en/blog/building-cura-lessons) serve suitable cases and follow-ups. Messaging and reminders support adherence. [**Remote monitoring**](https://www.who.int/news/item/23-09-2024-boosting-digital-health-can-help-prevent-millions-of-deaths-from-noncommunicable-diseases) detects change early. Analytics identify high-risk groups. A unified record protects information continuity.

The strength of primary care becomes especially visible in [**chronic disease**](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/noncommunicable-diseases). A person living with [**diabetes**](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/diabetes) needs a long journey that includes testing, medication, nutrition, physical activity, and follow-up. One team leading this journey converts separate visits into one plan.

[**Investment in primary care**](https://www.who.int/health-topics/universal-health-coverage) improves access, strengthens prevention, organizes referrals, and reduces pressure on hospitals. It also brings the system closer to citizens and increases its ability to intervene at the right time.

Every health transformation seeking greater efficiency needs primary care with the tools, data, and responsibility required to lead the journey.
