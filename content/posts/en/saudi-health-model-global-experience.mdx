Every health system reflects its country's history, population, economy, and institutions. This is why Saudi Arabia is building its model from mechanisms that have proven effective in international experience, then deploying them within a local context.

The idea of health clusters with population-based responsibility appears in several systems around the world. The role of primary care in organizing the patient journey is prominent in the [**UK system**](https://www.england.nhs.uk/integratedcare/what-is-integrated-care/). Population-based financing and strategic purchasing of care appear in different forms in [**Canada**](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/canada-health-care-system.html), [**Australia**](https://www.health.gov.au/our-work/phn/what-PHNs-are), and the [**United States**](https://www.cms.gov/priorities-innovation-key-concepts-accountable-care-accountable-care-organizations).

Saudi Arabia combines these mechanisms inside a model that serves its own priorities.

The state guarantees [**universal coverage**](https://www.who.int/ar/health-topics/universal-health-coverage) for citizens. [**Health clusters**](https://www.health.sa/ar/clusters/list) manage care within defined geographic areas. The [**funding body purchases services**](https://www.cnhi.gov.sa/ar/About/Pages/default.aspx). The Ministry of Health focuses on regulation and oversight. The [**Saudi Model of Care**](https://www.health.sa/ar/model-of-care) gives prevention, primary care, and digital services a central role.

The value of this approach comes from choosing mechanisms deliberately.

Funding distribution requires accurate population data. The referral mechanism requires [**strong primary care**](https://www.who.int/ar/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/primary-health-care). Cluster independence requires financial and operational capabilities with [**clear governance**](https://www.health.sa/ar/model-of-care). [**Private sector purchasing**](https://www.who.int/activities/promoting-strategic-purchasing) requires contracts and quality indicators. [**Digital services**](https://www.waelkabli.com/ar/blog/ai-in-digital-health) require integration with the care journey.

Successfully transferring any experience depends on understanding why it worked in its home country, then creating the conditions for it to work locally.

The Saudi model has an important structural advantage: the ability to build a large national system while creating space for development across [**twenty health clusters**](https://www.health.sa/ar/clusters/list), each serving a different population with different needs.

This structure enables learning, benchmarking, and practice exchange. Over time, Saudi Arabia can produce health system knowledge worth sharing with other countries seeking universal coverage, efficiency, and digital transformation within a single unified system.
